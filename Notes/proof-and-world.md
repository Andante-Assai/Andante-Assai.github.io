A proof offers a clean kind of safety: the premises are explicit, every step can be checked, and the conclusion does not change with tone. The world rarely gives us its premises in full. It gives us measurements, error, and several models that do not quite agree.

## Rigor is not pretend precision

Real rigor includes honesty about scope. A theory can be extraordinarily effective at one scale and lose its meaning at another. Acknowledging that boundary does not weaken it; it makes the conclusion useful.

## Conflict is information

When two models disagree, we do not need to discard one immediately. The difference may come from an approximation, a scale of observation, or the fact that they answer different questions. Preserving the conflict often looks more like real scientific work than forcing an early unification.

A proof tells us that under these premises, the conclusion must follow. A model reminds us that whether the world accepts those premises is another question.
