Some problems are not too difficult; they have simply been forced into the wrong form. We turn relationships into sentences and compress change into definitions, until the result is grammatically correct but almost impossible to move around in the mind.

At that point, drawing is neither decoration nor the final step of an explanation. It can arrive before the answer and become part of the thinking itself.

## Give relationships a place

When a concept has position, direction, and distance, another kind of intuition appears. Nearness suggests connection, movement suggests change, and overlap shows two explanations competing for the same object. Relationships that take paragraphs to describe can become obvious at a glance.

## A sketch may be wrong

The value of a sketch is not that it predicts the correct answer. It exposes mistakes. A node that will not fit, an arrow forced to take a detour, or an empty space that cannot be named all reveal that the original understanding is missing something.

So when language begins to knot, I would rather draw a picture that can be disproved. The answer may not appear immediately, but the problem usually becomes a little more honest.
